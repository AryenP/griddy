Brownout

Grid-aware AI compute dispatcher · Cal Hacks 13.0, Palace of Fine Arts, San Francisco · Fri 23 – Sun 25 Oct 2026 · Team brief, rewritten Fri 9:00 PM after the reframe
The claim (verified). When the grid runs short, the plan for AI data centers is diesel generators: during Winter Storm Fern in January 2026, with over a million people out, the Energy Secretary authorized PJM, ERCOT and Duke to order data centers onto backup generators, citing 35 GW of diesel. Nobody asked them to shed compute, because no system exists to do it. Brownout is that system: it watches the grid live (real prices, forecasts, fuel mix, alerts), decides when and how every AI job runs so power goes to what needs it, executes on real hardware, asks job owners before a deadline slips, and proves the result with measured power.

Why this, why now

  • It's becoming law. Texas PUCT Docket 59220 (Jul 2026): co-located data centers must fully curtail on ERCOT's instruction within 30 minutes, uncompensated, with ~60 min notice. PJM (Jan 2026): new loads ≥50 MW without their own generation are curtailed first in emergencies. FERC show-cause orders (Jun 2026) on flexible large loads. EPRI estimates 10–30% peak-reduction potential.
  • Everyone is promising flexibility; nobody proves it from the rack. Emerald AI (Nvidia-backed) cut a GPU cluster 25% for 3 h in Phoenix (2025). Google signed ML-workload demand-response deals (Aug 2025). Google, Nvidia and Emerald formed an "AI Energy Management Alliance" (Sep 2026); OpenAI agreed to shed up to 1 GW in Georgia. Our differentiators: the agent layer (negotiation, approvals, honest shortfall) and independent verification.
  • The data is real and keyless. Verified Friday 8:50 PM from the venue: CAISO 5-minute prices (NP15 $58.10, SP15 $52.62/MWh at the time), 24-hour load forecast and fuel mix; ERCOT real-time frequency and demand. The dispatcher plans against what the grid is doing right now.
  • Demo on one MacBook, nothing simulated. Power is measured by macOS (powermetrics); workloads are a chat service (Ollama), a training job (PyTorch MPS, checkpointing) and a batch job. Scale is 5–40 W; the loop is the same at 50 MW and we say so.

The judge demo (one judge plays every role, ~4 min, no slides)

  1. Open on the dashboard header: the diesel line, and under it the quote from a Microsoft FDE or operator (collected Sat 10 AM). "Pick a grid."
  2. They pick the ISO and the policy (cost, carbon, balanced). Live prices with timestamps, the 24-hour price and carbon curves, the schedule painted on top: training in cheap overnight slots, batch in clean midday solar, chat always on. The solver's two-sentence explanation.
  3. They use the service from their phone; p95 latency, measured watts, $/h and gCO2/h all live.
  4. They're the utility: "grid alert, I need 30% off for twenty minutes," in their words. Agent reads back the plan and asks the training owner.
  5. They're the owner: they refuse in words ("has to finish by 9 AM Monday"). The solver re-plans with that constraint: "22%, not 30%, unless chat slows ~1 s. Approve?" They decide; watts drop; time-to-compliance in seconds; chat still fast.
  6. They move the grid: switch to carbon, or to ERCOT. The schedule repaints against the new live curve; the Mac follows.
  7. Impossible ask: 90%. "Floor with chat protected is N W (≈47%). Go dark on everything else, or accept 47%?" Shortfall logged.
  8. Receipts: training resumes from checkpoint; report: baseline vs event watts, % reduction, time-to-compliance, approvals with names and times; comparison vs dispatcher-off: $ and gCO2 saved, deadlines met; the audit trace for one decision.

The LLM looks optionalIt owns the messy input (an email with two constraints and a typo), the free-text negotiation, and the shortfall explanation. Test for every LLM call: "could a regex do this?" If yes, it moves to the controller.

After the trials the project was reframed from a facility throttle to a grid-aware dispatcher (live ISO data → optimizer → execution → measurement), which directly answers "a solver could do this": the solver is the product and the LLM only explains and negotiates. A second trial on a space variant (satellite flood-mapping jobs as the workloads, for the SpaceXSI track) went 6–0 for the base framing. Kept as a conditional: flood jobs become tenant workloads only if a pretrained Sen1Floods11 model runs live on the box by Sat 1 PM with under 2 h of work.

Machine, data and stack facts (verified Fri)

  • Grid data (keyless, verified from the venue): gridstatus — CAISO get_lmp(date="today", market="REAL_TIME_5_MIN", locations=[...]), get_load_forecast("today"), get_fuel_mix("today"); ERCOT get_real_time_system_conditions() (frequency, demand, capacity), get_spp; PJM get_lmp. Calls take 1–5 s; poll in the background. Carbon intensity is computed from the live fuel mix with cited emission factors.
  • Power on the Mac: sudo powermetrics --samplers cpu_power,gpu_power -i 1000 gives CPU, GPU, ANE and combined watts every second, reported by macOS, independent of our code. Label it "SoC power, measured by macOS". Calibrate per-job deltas; never type watts into config.
  • Workloads: chat via Ollama behind our proxy (concurrency = our semaphore); training on PyTorch MPS with checkpoints and cooperative pause; batch embeddings resumable on CPU or GPU. Knobs: run/pause, concurrency, threads, device. No clock lock or power limit on macOS.
  • Optimizer: OR-Tools CP-SAT over 96 fifteen-minute slots; 2 s time limit; on infeasibility a slack model returns the minimal relaxation (which deadline must slip, by how much) for the negotiator.
  • Request format: OpenADR 3.1 events (what utilities send) plus plain English.
  • Microsoft: pip install agent-framework (FoundryChatClient on Azure); App Insights via azd provision; Teams two-way needs a tenant admin, so the demo uses a web owner console and states "Teams via the M365 Agents SDK in production"; Content Safety shieldPrompt on inbound text.
