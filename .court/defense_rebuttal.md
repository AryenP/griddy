# Defense rebuttal

**1. Toy scale.** Conceded in part: tens of watts will not persuade anyone on size. The cure is to stop selling wattage and put a short "what changes at 100 MW" slide beside the meter, so the demo is read as proof of the control loop. Percent-of-load and the 47% floor are scale-free and should be the headline numbers.

**2. Self-authored baseline.** Partly right. Uniform throttling is a deliberately simple foil, so add a second baseline, a static priority list with no negotiation or verification, and publish all baseline settings. State plainly that this is a design comparison, not a benchmark against Emerald.

**3. Fragility.** Conceded. Venue Wi-Fi and a live LLM call are real risks. Cure: run the web console over a local hotspot or the box's own network, cache the plan read-back, and keep a one-keystroke fallback that skips to the next step. Sim mode is a labelled backup, not the demo.

**4. Lamp step.** Mostly conceded. A 1 Hz plug plus settling time makes "within seconds" marginal. Cure: rehearse and measure the actual response, promise only what was measured (for example "under 5 s"), use a 40 W or larger lamp so the step clears the noise, and cut step 4 first if time is short.

**5. Customer understanding.** Conceded. Regulation and press are market evidence, not operator evidence. Cure: talk to the FDEs on Saturday and put one concrete quote or constraint on the opening slide, such as which workloads they would never pause.

**6. Competition.** Emerald and Google prove demand, but the prosecution's real point is a Microsoft-native stack. Cure: use the Foundry client path in the demo if time allows, and name Entra agent identity and Teams approval as the next adapters. Do not claim parity with incumbents.

**7. LLM as decoration.** Only partly right. The judge gives plain-English requests and refusals, and the agent turns them into constraints, re-plans and explains the shortfall; a solver cannot parse "cut 30% for ten minutes" or negotiate. Cure: make the free-text step prominent in the demo, so the LLM visibly earns its place.

**8. Production gap.** Conceded for scheduling, multi-tenancy and facility metering; none is built. The defensible claim is narrower: the controller interface, allowlist, approval gate and audit log are real and tested. Cure: show the audit log live and put the gaps on a plainly labelled roadmap slide rather than implying coverage.

**Net.** Charges 3, 5 and 8 are real and fixable by the changes above. Charges 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 are about framing and calibration, not a flaw in the idea.
